    How I work

    Nothing fancy — just a clear process that keeps things moving.

    1) Figure out what's broken
    • Look at how people actually use the site
    • Find the spots where they get confused or bounce
    • Agree on what "done" looks like before writing a line of code
    2) Build it right
    • Clean components that won't turn into spaghetti later
    • Responsive layouts that look good on everything
    • CTAs and copy that actually make people want to click
    3) Polish and ship
    • Make it fast — not just "passes Lighthouse" fast, actually fast
    • Test forms, edge cases, and weird screen sizes
    • QA it myself before you ever see it

    A bit about me

    The short version of how I got here.

    I got into web development the way most people do — messing around, breaking things, and slowly figuring out how to put them back together. That was about ten years ago. Since then I've worked on everything from quick marketing sites to complex web apps with lots of moving parts.

    What keeps me going is the craft. I genuinely enjoy figuring out why a layout feels off by 2 pixels, or why a form is making people give up halfway through. I'm not just writing code — I'm thinking about the person who has to use what I build.
